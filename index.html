A note on our name לְדוֹר וָדוֹר

The name L’Dor Vador translates from Hebrew as “from generation to generation.” It embodies the core of Jewish continuity: that profound, sentimental tie connecting our ancestors to their descendants via memory and tradition. It signifies our duty to transmit our history and values.

At L’Dor Vador Travel, this sentiment defines our mission: we create journeys that bridge the gap between travelers and the roots of Jewish existence in Curaçao, providing an opportunity to enter the transatlantic narrative and discover your own role within it.

Discover Curaçao

A vibrant island in the West Indies, and an autonomous country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands.

Situated in the southern Caribbean, between Aruba and Bonaire and just 40 miles off the coast of Venezuela, Curaçao is home to approximately 158,000 people. It is a welcoming, multilingual society where Papiamentu, Dutch, English, and Spanish are widely spoken. Its established institutions, modern infrastructure, and constitutional ties to the Kingdom contribute to an environment in which travelers can feel at ease.

Remarkably accessible

Nonstop flights connect the island with major US gateways including Miami, New York, Newark, Atlanta, Chicago and Charlotte, plus Toronto and Montreal in Canada, placing Curaçao within a five-hour flight of many major North American cities. The island is also a prominent Caribbean cruise destination. Hundreds of cruise ships call at Willemstad each year, docking within walking distance of its historic center; in 2025 alone, Curaçao welcomed more than 880,000 cruise passengers.

An extraordinary legacy

Beyond its clear waters, dramatic coastline, rugged natural landscapes, and dozens of inviting beaches, Curaçao holds an extraordinary cultural legacy. Its capital, Willemstad, is a UNESCO World Heritage city known for its colorful architecture, historic harbor, and the swinging Queen Emma Bridge. It is also home to the Snoa, the oldest synagogue in continuous use in the Americas, and Beth Haim Bleinheim, the oldest walled-in Jewish cemetery in the Americas.

Together, these places reveal Curaçao as something many travelers never expected to find: one of the most important and enduring centers of Jewish life in the Caribbean.
